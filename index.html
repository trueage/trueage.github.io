❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Learn more about exact age mathematics, leap year corrections, and astronomical timekeeping.

How does TrueAge calculate exact chronological age?

TrueAge calculates exact age using calendar-accurate algorithms that account for varying days in each month and leap years (such as February 29th). It computes the difference between your birth moment and the exact current system time down to the second.

How does leap year affect age calculations?

A standard Gregorian year has 365 days, while a leap year has 366 days. TrueAge's mathematical engine determines whether your elapsed periods crossed a leap day (Feb 29) and dynamically adjusts the month length when calculating exact elapsed days, months, and years.

What is a 10,000th Day and 1 Billion Seconds milestone?

Your 10,000th day occurs when you are approximately 27 years and 4.5 months old. One billion seconds alive occurs around age 31 years, 8 months, and 19 days. TrueAge calculates the precise calendar date and time for each milestone.

How is age calculated on other planets like Mars or Jupiter?

Planetary age is determined by each planet's orbital period around the Sun relative to Earth. For instance, Mars takes 1.8808 Earth years to complete one orbit, so a 30-year-old on Earth is approximately 15.95 Martian years old.

What is a half-birthday?

A half-birthday occurs exactly 6 calendar months from your birth date. It serves as an enjoyable mid-year milestone celebration point.

Is my birth date stored on any server?

No. TrueAge is 100% client-side. All date calculations and processing occur entirely inside your browser sandbox. No personal data or birth details are ever sent to any remote server.

The Science of Chronological Age & Astronomical Timekeeping

Chronological age measures the duration of time elapsed from a person's moment of birth to the current instant. While civil time uses simple yearly anniversaries, true biological and mathematical age is continuous and affected by the complexities of the Gregorian calendar system.

Gregorian Drift and Leap Year Corrections

The Earth requires approximately 365.2422 solar days to complete one full revolution around the Sun (a tropical year). To account for this fractional day, the Gregorian calendar adds a leap day on February 29th every 4 years, omitting years divisible by 100 unless also divisible by 400. TrueAge dynamically evaluates every month boundary to ensure precise year-month-day decomposition.

Planetary Orbital Periods

A year is physically defined as one orbital revolution of a celestial body around its host star. Because orbital distances follow Kepler's laws of planetary motion, inner planets like Mercury orbit rapidly in just 87.97 Earth days, while outer giants like Jupiter require nearly 12 Earth years. TrueAge provides exact astronomical translations of your lifespan across all major planets in the solar system.
